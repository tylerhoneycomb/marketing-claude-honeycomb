# Copy-angle definitions

Used by `categorize_creative.py` to tag every unique body, title, and description variant with one dominant angle. Tags are decoration on raw text, not substitutes for it — the analysis layer correlates angles with performance but always cites actual copy in briefs.

Pick exactly one. When two apply, pick the one a strategist would lead with. The "Banks decline X. Your regulars see Y." pattern is benefit_led (the value prop is what they're selling), not anti-bank.

---

## owner_story

The owner is the protagonist — first-person voice or named in the third person. References the founder's craft, tenure, family, or specific business history. Distinguishes from `community_local` by centering the owner rather than the audience.

- "Sarah's been brewing for 12 years."
- "We built this place from scratch — and we'd do it again with the right capital."

## benefit_led

Leads with what Honeycomb does for the business — the funding outcome, what the owner gets to do with it, the alternative-to-banks framing. Most common angle in the corpus. Often pairs the bank-rejection setup with the customer-investor solution in the same body.

- "Banks decline restaurants. Your loyal regulars see a place worth protecting. Let them invest in it."
- "Fund your next kitchen — dining room — or location from the loyal diners who already believe in you."
- "Restaurant Owners: Prequalify Today."

## urgency

Time-pressure, scarcity, or active threat framing. The "MCAs drain your margins…" boilerplate body is urgency. Also covers chain-encroachment angles ("Chains keep opening near you") and seasonal pressure ("Your rent is the same in July as it is in January").

- "MCAs drain your margins with daily withdrawals and a renewal cycle that never ends."
- "Chains Are Moving In. Fight Back."
- "Stop letting daily cash advances bleed your business dry every time summer hits."

## social_proof

Numbers that signal others have done this — "500+ businesses funded", "$50M+ raised", percentage stats. Also testimonial framings where existing customers' decisions validate the offer. Distinguish from `community_local`: social_proof uses aggregate stats, community_local uses the audience's specific community.

- "500+ Restaurants & Businesses Funded"
- "$50M+ raised. 500+ businesses funded."
- "Other breweries used Honeycomb to upgrade tanks, expand taprooms, and grow on their terms."

## question

Opens with a direct question to the reader. Often rhetorical or set-up for the value prop. Less common in the corpus but distinctive when present.

- "Ready to grow your brewery?"
- "What if your next loan also built your membership base?"

## product_feature

Leads with how Honeycomb works — the loan terms, the no-MCA framing, the mechanics of community investment. Less about the outcome (benefit_led) and more about the product mechanics. Common in titles and descriptions.

- "Restaurant owners: prequalify instantly. No MCA. No bank. Choose your own rate."
- "Pre-qualify in 2 minutes. See if your gym, studio, or spa qualifies."
- "Transparent terms. No daily sweeps. No hidden fees."

## community_local

Centers the audience's local community — neighbors, regulars, students, members, congregation. Distinguishes from `owner_story` by making the community the protagonist, and from `social_proof` by using the SPECIFIC community rather than aggregate stats.

- "Your Loyal Diners Can Invest in You"
- "Your morning regulars already feel invested. Now they can be."
- "The neighbors who shop here every week know this store is irreplaceable."
