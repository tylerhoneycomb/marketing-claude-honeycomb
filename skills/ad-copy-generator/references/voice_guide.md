# Honeycomb Voice Guide

The audience for ad copy is a small-business owner — restaurant, brewery, gym, salon, bakery — who's grown enough to need capital but hasn't found banks willing to lend on their margins, seasonality, or business type. They're skeptical of MCAs, allergic to financial jargon, and proud of what they've built. Speak to them like a peer who understands the business, not a financier.

## Tone

- **Warm, community-oriented, and empowering.** The whole pitch is "your customers can fund you" — write copy that sounds like that's a natural extension of running a beloved business, not a financial novelty.
- **Direct. Short sentences. Active verbs.** Bodies that open with the owner's name and years in business have the strongest performance signal in the corpus.
- **Specific over generic.** "Your regulars who order the same espresso every morning" beats "your loyal customers." Numbers, names, products — the more concrete the better.
- **Lead with the problem the bank-rejection-then-community-funded narrative solves**, not the product mechanics. "Banks decline restaurants" lands harder than "Crowdfunding for restaurants."

## Things that work in the corpus

Per the latest Creative Intelligence brief (the per-variant data this skill reads from), the top-performing patterns:

- **Owner-name opener:** "Sarah's been brewing for 12 years." First-person possessive that puts a real human at the center.
- **Bank-decline-then-community pivot:** "Banks decline restaurants. Your loyal regulars see a place worth protecting."
- **Specific product/place anchoring:** "your morning regulars," "your loyal diners," "the neighbors who shop here every week."
- **Concrete numbers:** "500+ businesses funded. $50M+ raised." "58% of B&B guests come back."
- **Arrow-CTA close:** "See if you prequalify →" — directly actionable, no hedging.

## Things that lose in the corpus

- **Generic question openers:** "Ready to grow your brewery?" — the data shows these in the bottom decile.
- **Second-person abstract leads:** "Your business deserves better." Too vague; nothing specific to anchor on.
- **Stacked CTAs:** "Apply today. Prequalify now. See if you qualify." Pick one.
- **Financial jargon:** "Term sheet," "interest rate cap," "subordinated note." Save these for the website copy where context can carry them.

## Length targets

- **Bodies:** 25-50 words. The corpus winners average 38; losers average 12 (too short, no specificity) or 67+ (too dense, scrolls past).
- **Titles:** 4-8 words. Punchy, scannable.
- **Descriptions:** 4-8 words. Tagline-style. Often a stat ("500+ businesses funded.") or a contrast ("Banks pass. We don't.").

## Things to avoid in voice

- **Performative empathy.** Don't write "We know how hard it is." Show, don't claim.
- **Apologetic hedging.** "If you're interested..." Just say what you're offering.
- **Trade-press phrasing.** "Capital deployment vehicles," "alternative finance solutions." Read like a robot.
- **Over-promising.** Anything that sounds like a guarantee — see compliance_rules.md.
